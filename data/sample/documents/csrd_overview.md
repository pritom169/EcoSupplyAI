# Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) - Overview

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) is a landmark piece of European Union legislation that significantly expands the scope and depth of sustainability reporting requirements for companies operating within the EU. Adopted by the European Parliament and Council in November 2022, the CSRD replaces and strengthens the earlier Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD). Under the CSRD, approximately 50,000 companies are required to report detailed sustainability information according to the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), compared to roughly 11,700 companies under the previous NFRD regime. The directive applies to all large EU companies, listed SMEs (with certain opt-out provisions), and non-EU companies generating over EUR 150 million in EU net turnover with at least one EU subsidiary or branch.

## Double Materiality

The CSRD introduces the concept of "double materiality," requiring companies to report not only on how sustainability issues affect their financial performance (financial materiality) but also on how their operations impact people and the environment (impact materiality). Companies must disclose information across a comprehensive set of Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) topics, including climate change mitigation and adaptation, biodiversity, resource use, workforce conditions, human rights in the value chain, and anti-corruption measures. All reported sustainability information must be subject to limited assurance by an independent auditor, with the EU considering a transition to reasonable assurance in the future. Reports must be published in a machine-readable digital format (XHTML with iXBRL tagging) as part of the company's annual management report.

## Phase-In Timeline

The CSRD is being phased in over several reporting years:

- **Wave 1 (FY2024)**: Companies already subject to the NFRD must comply.
- **Wave 2 (FY2025)**: Other large companies meeting at least two of three thresholds: over 250 employees, EUR 50 million in net turnover, or EUR 25 million in total assets.
- **Wave 3 (FY2026)**: Listed SMEs begin reporting, with the option to delay until FY2028.
- **Wave 4 (FY2028)**: Non-EU companies with significant EU activity must comply.

Companies should begin their readiness assessments immediately, as the ESRS standards require granular data collection across the entire value chain, including Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions, supply chain due diligence findings, and transition plans aligned with the Paris Agreement's 1.5-degree target.
